The Artist

Turar Turganalieva

Member of the Union of Artists of the Kyrgyz Republic. She calls her work sacred embroidery: ornament that is not invented but received — drawn in soap on cloth, then followed, stitch by stitch, wherever it leads.

Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan

Two lives

Turar was born in Bishkek; her family’s roots lie on the shores of Issyk-Kul. Her mother was an artist, and embroidery lived in the house like life itself: as a girl she helped her mother every day, watched her grandmother work, and thought nothing of it — she simply went and helped.

She herself trained as an economist and spent more than twenty years as an accountant. When her mother died, embroidery became, for a while, a living: foreigners carried the works out of Bishkek one after another. Then she told herself: enough chasing money. A name must be made. A trace must be left. At forty, she walked away from the profession.

Eight years at the window

One can only work by daylight: the lamp lies about the colour of thread. At eight in the morning she sat down by the window — and rose at one or two at night. “Like Papa Carlo,” eight years running.

The family looked at her sideways: two diplomas, and there you sit with a needle. “Say what you like — I will do what is mine.” At forty-eight the Union of Artists of Kyrgyzstan admitted the self-taught master; of her works people said: they graduate from the academy and cannot do this.

“Embroidery is my meditation. People sit down on purpose to meditate; I pick up the needle.”

The practice

She does not plan and makes no sketches. She takes the cloth, draws with an ordinary bar of soap — and from there intuition leads. As she works she talks with herself: it is, she says, the second “I”.

If the soul aches, she will never sit down to work: everything alive in the maker passes into the thread. A large work she can finish in a week of unbroken days; others lie for years, until she sees what they were missing.

A language of signs

She does not call her ornament Kyrgyz alone: Scandinavians, Siberians, Mongolians recognise their own in it. “It belongs to the whole world. As they say — the ancestor was one.”

The dragon: the companion angel born with every person, guarding them to the end. The rainbow: seven colours gathering into white — purity. The seed: the birth of the Universe. The works’ purpose: to cleanse, to heal, to guard.

“They are priceless. Such works simply do not exist elsewhere — they are the heritage of the country.”

Recognition

Exhibitions in Bishkek, a solo exhibition in Moscow, works in private collections from Germany to America. She does not look for students — they find her, by word of mouth.

The atelier

Between large works Turar makes one-of-a-kind garments: hand-quilted coats, chapans cut from century-old cloth, embroidered kurak jackets. Natural fibres only; each piece is made once and never repeated.
